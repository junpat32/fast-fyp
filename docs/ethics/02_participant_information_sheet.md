## Testing FAST: can young adults understand a money-risk app?

You are invited to take part in a study for my Final Year Project at Multimedia University.
Please read this sheet before deciding. You can keep it.

**Who is doing the study?** Patrick Wong Jun Wen, a final-year BSc Computer Science (AI)
student, [faculty], Multimedia University, supervised by [supervisor's name].

**What is the study about?** I have built a prototype mobile app, FAST, that explains a person's
money risk in simple words: whether their debts are at a safe level, how things might change in
the next few months and why, and a small game where a home and garden grow as savings build up. I
want to find out whether people can **understand** what the app shows and find it **easy to use**.
**The app is being tested, not you.**

**Who can take part?** Anyone aged **18 to 35**, living in Malaysia, who can read English and use a
smartphone.

**What will I do?** One session of about **50 minutes**, **in person** in a quiet room on campus
or **online** by video call, whichever you prefer. You will:
1. answer a few general questions (age group, whether you study or work, whether you use money apps);
2. set up the app as a new user would, typing in the **made-up details on a card** I give you
   (about 5 minutes);
3. use the app, looking at **made-up people** ("personas") and their made-up finances, and trying a
   few things such as recording a made-up expense. In person you
   use a phone I provide. Online you use your own phone: you install the free **Expo Go** app, open
   FAST from a link I send, and sign in with made-up accounts I make for you (I delete them after
   the session);
4. answer some questions about what the app showed, how easy it was to use, and whether an app like
   it would be useful to you, on an online form.

**Will you ask about my own money?** **No.** You will never be asked about your own income,
savings, debts or bank details, and you should not enter any. Everything in the app is invented,
including the details you type in when you set up the app.

**Do I have to take part?** No. It is completely up to you. Saying no, or stopping halfway, has no
effect on your studies, grades, or how I treat you. You can skip any question.

**Can I change my mind later?** Yes. You can stop at any time without giving a reason. You can ask
me to remove your answers until **[date, e.g. 11 Jan 2027]**. After that, answers are combined and
can no longer be traced to you.

**Are there any risks?** Very few. Some personas have debts or money problems, which may remind you
of real worries. If anything feels uncomfortable, you can skip it or stop. Free, confidential help
with money and debt is available from **AKPK** (Agensi Kaunseling dan Pengurusan Kredit, set up by
Bank Negara Malaysia): [AKPK contact details, checked at the time of printing].

**Are there any benefits?** No direct benefit, though you may learn something about debt ratios and
emergency savings. There is no payment or gift.

**What happens to my answers?**
- Your answers are labelled with a **code** (for example P07), never your name. The online form
  does not ask for your name or email.
- Your consent is kept **separately** from your answers.
- Nothing is audio-, video- or screen-recorded, online sessions included.
- Only I and my supervisor see the answers. The FYP report shows only group results and anonymous
  comments that cannot identify you.
- Answers are deleted at the end of the project, when my final FYP result is released.
- This follows Malaysia's Personal Data Protection Act 2010.

**The app's AI assistant** sends questions you type, with the made-up person's figures, to an
external AI service (Groq). Please **do not type any personal information** into it.

**Has the study been approved?** It has been reviewed by [MMU committee name], reference
[number].

**Questions or concerns?** Patrick Wong Jun Wen, [email], [phone]. Supervisor: [name], [email].
If you have concerns about how the study is run, you can contact [MMU committee contact].

Thank you for reading this.
